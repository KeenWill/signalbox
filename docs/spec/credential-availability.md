# Credential availability

This page was introduced and verified through PR #453
(`agent/credential-pools-grammar`).

It owns one thing: what a credential-pool selection attempt can end as, and what
every other page must say about each ending. The pool grammar, membership
ranking, trigger vocabulary, and admission rules are owned by
[credential pools and selection](configuration-and-credentials.md#credential-pools-and-selection);
the successor call's own mechanics by
[availability successor calls](model-call-execution.md#availability-successor-calls);
the phase algebra by
[turn lifecycle and scheduling](turn-lifecycle-and-scheduling.md#turns-states-and-the-single-active-slot);
the storage protocol by
[persistence protocol](persistence-protocol.md#lock-protocol); the entry
vocabulary by
[sessions and the transcript](sessions-and-transcript.md#when-entries-come-to-exist);
the wire shapes by
[process protocol](process-protocol.md#credential-pool-preparation-failure); and
the evidence algebra by
[terminal evidence](runtime-substrate.md#terminal-evidence).

## The credential-availability machine

This build composes no credential pool. Every model call resolves the single
credential reference its session pinned at creation, and every known failure
terminalizes its turn — the last row of the table below, reached by the first of
its four disjoint reasons.

### Committed unimplemented functionality — the credential-availability machine

No present composition resolves a pool, selects a member, reserves capacity,
parks a turn, or prepares a successor. What follows is the contract the
implementing children satisfy, and the table is its whole normative statement.

Rather than let each page describe this machine from its own side, this contract
states the complete set of endings once and gives every projection a column.
**Every credential-pool selection attempt ends in exactly one of the seven rows
below, and each row fixes every projection of that ending that any specification
page states.** A page may name the column it projects and link here; it may not
author a competing account of a row.

The rows partition on two questions asked in order — did the traversal select a
member, and if not, was anything skipped only for its concurrency bound — and
then on the pool's `on_pool_exhausted` value and whether the turn had already
issued a call. Nothing else divides them, which is why there are seven and not
more.

`park` and `fail` have their semantics here and nowhere else. `park` selects
between `contended-wait` and `exhausted-wait`; `fail` selects between
`pre-call fail` and `post-failure fail`. Neither value has any other effect, and
neither appears in a row it does not name.

Two consequences of that are stated here because three pages previously reached
opposite answers on them. First, **a contended wait that becomes exhausted
re-runs `on_pool_exhausted` rather than staying parked.** When a woken contended
waiter finds that every formerly bounded candidate is now durably excluded,
contention is over and the pool is exhausted; the transaction that observes this
moves the turn to `exhausted-wait` under `park` and to `pre-call fail` or
`post-failure fail` under `fail`, taking the predecessor's provider cause where
the chain had issued a call and `credential_pool_exhausted` where it had not. A
`fail` pool that happened to contend first therefore terminalizes, rather than
parking indefinitely under a policy it never selected. Second, **releasing a
parked wait ends that chain**: the resumed turn starts a fresh chain and
recomputes admission, while every exclusion the wait carried stays in force for
the remainder of the turn, so nothing the wait recorded is readmitted by the
release.

Two exceptions to the row partition, and only these two. A turn whose stop was
requested reaches `terminal` from any row that would otherwise have continued,
because a cancelled attempt admits no successor and a cancelled wait is closed
rather than woken. And a delivery-layer credential failure — a rejected
daemon-owned OAuth refresh, or a credential-home identity that failed its walk —
never enters this machine at all: it occurs before any provider request, is
typed as its own refresh or credential-home failure, and quarantines the profile
directly under
[credential deliveries](configuration-and-credentials.md#credential-deliveries).
The pool's trigger policy does not see it, and no row above describes it.

Why the machine is stated as one table rather than as each page's own paragraph:
it was tried the other way first, and the result was that seven pages each owned
a closed inventory over the same seven endings. A new ending on one page left
six closed inventories silently wrong, and the disagreement surfaced only when a
reviewer happened to read two pages together — one pair per round, for
forty-three rounds. Five statements ended up written out in full on three or
more pages, and the two that disagreed about a `fail` pool's exhausted wait had
been edited in different months by authors who each believed they owned it. The
table makes that class unwritable rather than merely discouraged: a missing
projection is a visibly empty cell rather than a cross-page discovery, the grid
is bounded at seven rows by seven columns, and **the row is the unit of edit**,
so changing an ending's terminal evidence edits its transcript producer and its
wire projection in the same place and the same commit.

No cell may be left blank. Where a projection does not apply to a row, the cell
says so and says why, because a blank cell cannot be told apart from one nobody
has filled in yet — which is the exact failure the table replaces.

## Derived views

Every other page's treatment of this machine is a derived view: a sentence
naming the column that page projects, plus a link here. A derived view may
restate a cell's content for local readability, and must not extend it. Where a
derived view and a row disagree, the row governs and the derived view is the
defect.

Each projection below is a column above, and names the page that owns it:

- Turn phase, attempt disposition, and wake conditions —
  [turn lifecycle and scheduling](turn-lifecycle-and-scheduling.md#turns-states-and-the-single-active-slot).
- Continuation origins, durable record shapes, and lock order —
  [persistence protocol](persistence-protocol.md#lock-protocol).
- Transcript producers and semantic entries —
  [sessions and the transcript](sessions-and-transcript.md#when-entries-come-to-exist).
- Snapshot turn state, live events, and `rejected.detail` —
  [process protocol](process-protocol.md#credential-pool-preparation-failure),
  with the operator clear surface at
  [credential-exclusion administration](process-protocol.md#credential-exclusion-administration).
- Terminal evidence and cause —
  [terminal evidence](runtime-substrate.md#terminal-evidence) for the evidence
  algebra, and [terminal outcomes](model-call-execution.md#terminal-outcomes)
  for the outcome each one derives.
- Pool grammar, ranking, and admission —
  [credential pools and selection](configuration-and-credentials.md#credential-pools-and-selection).

`scripts/check_availability_projections.py` gates both halves of this: that no
cell is empty, and that no page states one of this machine's projections without
linking here.
